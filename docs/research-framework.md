# Research Framework

## Working proposition

AI and quantum computing may create workforce transitions that are not captured well by traditional role descriptions alone.

The research therefore treats workforce readiness as a socio-technical problem involving:

- individual skills and human capital
- technology maturity
- organizational processes
- learning and reskilling mechanisms
- labor-market signals
- strategic workforce planning

## Analytical chain

**Technology change → task/role change → capability change → human-capital response → organizational readiness**

The technical prototype currently addresses the middle of this chain by extracting capability signals from job descriptions.

## What the prototype can and cannot establish

It can:

- identify explicit skill mentions
- group skills into a predefined taxonomy
- compare capability presence across roles
- provide a reproducible baseline for later semantic methods

It cannot currently:

- infer true skill proficiency
- establish causal relationships
- measure labor-market demand
- determine which skill is strategically most valuable
- make candidate or employment decisions

## Next methodological step

Build an evaluation set in which human reviewers label a sample of job descriptions for:

1. explicit skills
2. implied capabilities
3. capability category
4. confidence/ambiguity

Then compare the deterministic baseline against semantic and LLM-assisted approaches using transparent evaluation criteria.
